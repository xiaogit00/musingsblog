A large part of what drives my curiosity about the markets nowadays is understanding where exactly I got it wrong, as well as what its current status is. Like, does my understanding of the market tally with existing data? What is the true state of things? How can I even begin to understand and make sense of the scale and size of things, what they are, and how they would go? 

It's obvious that, within this gravity defying moment, I have missed out on a whole lot of opportunities. Well, for one, if I had bought certain stocks in 2020, just 6 years ago, I could have been a millionaire. The opportunities exist, just as they do now. My prime mistake was taking a conservative stance and having an inaccurate forecast that the markets are going to go into recession because that's how things have always been. They go up, and then they come down. But what's fascinating and unexpected is that just as it's unattractive to exit too late and to suffer a wipe out for a prolonged period of time (and have your capital locked in), it's equally unattractive to exit too early and miss out on the sweetness of a late stage frothy bubbly stock market. 

Am I wrong, or are the markets just irrational for a long time? 

How do you seek out truth from which you form your evaluations? 

How do you even start building out a picture of 'value'? And to be able to separate value from the top layer speculative value that is reflected in price? And to then be able to see that the speculative value will reflexively also act as a second order supply of value perhaps somewhere down the road? 

The thing is, it's important to remind myself that all these things are probabilistic. I took an implicit bet somewhere around 5 years ago. My bet was that: the markets will behave like it did historically, of crashing every 10 odd years or so, where during the crashes, its value might contract for more than 40% at times, for a long-ish period of time for about a year or so. 

I only wanted to enter during recessions. 

Well, how true is that historically? Let's review that thesis again.
